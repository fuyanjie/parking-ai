Research Overview

Rapid urbanization in Sun Belt cities like Phoenix has created a critical gap between parking demand and supply. Downtown utilization rates exceed 94%, and projections indicate a deficit of 12,400+ spaces by 2030. Traditional planning methods — relying on static surveys and manual assessment — fail to capture the dynamic, multi-dimensional nature of urban parking demand. This project develops a multi-agent AI system that autonomously integrates heterogeneous urban data sources, performs spatial analysis across 12 dimensions, checks policy compliance, stress-tests against economic scenarios, and generates investment-ready recommendations for new parking infrastructure.

🎯 Research Objectives

  • Design a multi-agent architecture for autonomous urban data integration and spatial reasoning
  • Develop a 12-dimension scoring model that quantifies parking site suitability across traffic, demographics, land use, transit access, and policy constraints
  • Build a supply-demand gap analysis framework integrating OSM parking, Phoenix street meters, Census commuting data, and ADOT traffic volumes
  • Create a policy compliance agent that validates recommendations against zoning, ADA, environmental, and municipal codes
  • Implement policy stress testing with Monte Carlo simulation across economic, demographic, and climate scenarios
  • Build an interactive decision-support tool with conversational AI for urban planners and policymakers

💡 Key Contributions

  • 6-Agent Pipeline — Traffic, POI, Parking, Transit, Demographics, and Policy agents collaborate in an 8-step pipeline with chain-of-thought reasoning
  • 9-Source Data Fusion — Integrates OSM, Census ACS (B01003, B08301, B19013, B25044), Phoenix Open Data (meters + zoning), ADOT AADT, Valley Metro GTFS, and FEMA flood maps — all in-browser without backend
  • Supply Estimation Model — 3.5 km radius analysis combining OSM parking facilities, Phoenix meter database, and area-calibrated baseline estimates
  • Policy Stress Testing — 8 parking policies evaluated under 5 pressure scenarios (economic recession, population surge, autonomous vehicles, climate events, transit expansion)
  • Explainable AI — Full chain-of-thought reasoning traces, rejected-site rationale, before/after coverage comparison, and sensitivity tornado charts
  • Conversational Control — LLM agent (Llama 3.3 70B via Groq) understands natural language to adjust weights, toggle scenarios, run pipeline, and answer analytical questions

Methodology

📐 Supply Estimation Model

For each candidate site, existing parking supply is estimated within a 3.5 km radius using three complementary data layers:

  • OSM Parking Facilities — All amenity=parking features with capacity tags (when available) or estimated at 150 spaces per facility
  • Phoenix Street Meters — Official parking meter locations from the City of Phoenix ArcGIS REST API, each counted as 1 space
  • Baseline Estimate — Area-calibrated estimate using known infrastructure density (50-300 spaces/km² depending on zoning)
  • Formula: Supply = OSM_spaces + Meter_count + Baseline × area_factor

📈 Demand Estimation Model

Parking demand is modeled as a function of traffic volume, population density, commercial activity, and commute patterns:

  • Traffic Component — AADT volume × parking generation rate (5-8% of daily traffic)
  • Population Component — Resident density × vehicle ownership rate (from Census B25044)
  • Commercial Component — POI density × category-specific parking demand factors
  • Drive-Alone Rate — Census B08301 commuting data by tract
  • Formula: Demand = α·Traffic + β·Population + γ·Commercial + δ·Events

🧮 Multi-Criteria Scoring (12 Dimensions)

Each candidate site is scored across 5 weighted dimensions (user-adjustable), with 12 underlying sub-criteria:

  • Traffic Score (0-100) — AADT volume, congestion index, intersection density
  • Gap Score (0-100) — Supply-demand deficit, utilization rate, overflow frequency
  • Growth Score (0-100) — Population growth rate, new building permits, land value appreciation
  • POI Score (0-100) — Retail/dining/medical/entertainment density within 1 km
  • Transit Score (0-100) — Distance to nearest rail stop, bus route coverage, park-and-ride potential

Final score: S = w&sub1;·Traffic + w&sub2;·Gap + w&sub3;·Growth + w&sub4;·POI + w&sub5;·Transit

📋 Policy Compliance Agent

Before final recommendation, each site passes through an automated policy check against:

  • Zoning Compliance — Verifies land use designation permits parking development (C-2, C-3, A-1/A-2 zones)
  • Environmental Review — Checks proximity to protected areas, flood zones, and heritage sites
  • ADA Accessibility — Ensures minimum accessible parking ratios per ADA standards
  • Municipal Code — Validates setback requirements, height limits, and stormwater management

🔬 Location-Aware Policy Stress Testing

8 policies evaluated under 10 pressure scenarios (7 individual + 3 compound) with zone-specific modifiers:

  • Zone Classification — Each site is classified by location: Downtown, Midtown, Suburban-Growth, Transit-Adjacent, or Outer Ring. Pressure effects vary by zone (e.g., Remote Work Surge reduces downtown traffic by 45% but only 15% in suburbs)
  • Individual Pressures (7) — Population Boom, Remote Work Surge, Gas Spike, Recession, Extreme Heat, EV Adoption Surge, Autonomous Vehicles — each with zone-specific multipliers
  • Compound Pressures (3) — Green Future (Gas+EV+Heat), Tech Disruption (Remote+AV), Growth Under Heat (Pop Boom+Heat) — combined effects stack non-linearly
  • Soft Flood Penalty — Flood-zone sites receive −30% gap score + 15% cost increase for mitigation instead of hard exclusion
  • Resilience Metric — Average absolute score swing across all 10 scenarios identifies which policy best withstands compound shocks

📊 Monte Carlo ROI Simulation

Investment returns are modeled using stochastic simulation to capture uncertainty:

  • Method — Box-Muller normal distribution, 5,000 iterations per site
  • Variables — Utilization rate (σ=15%), construction cost (σ=20%), revenue per space (σ=10%)
  • Output — P10/P50/P90 ROI distribution, probability of positive return, histogram with confidence intervals
  • Sensitivity — Tornado chart showing score impact when each dimension weight is minimized vs maximized

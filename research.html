AI-Driven Urban Parking Site Selection

Leveraging multi-agent AI systems, real-time urban data integration, and spatial optimization to address the growing parking infrastructure deficit in rapidly expanding metropolitan areas.

Research Overview

Rapid urbanization in Sun Belt cities like Phoenix has created a critical gap between parking demand and supply. Downtown utilization rates exceed 94%, and projections indicate a deficit of 12,400+ spaces by 2030. Traditional planning methods — relying on static surveys and manual assessment — fail to capture the dynamic, multi-dimensional nature of urban parking demand. This project develops a multi-agent AI system that autonomously integrates heterogeneous urban data sources, performs spatial analysis across 12 dimensions, and generates investment-ready recommendations for new parking infrastructure.

🎯 Research Objectives

  • Design a multi-agent architecture for autonomous urban data integration and spatial reasoning
  • Develop a 12-dimension scoring model that quantifies parking site suitability across traffic, demographics, land use, transit access, and policy constraints
  • Create a supply-demand gap analysis framework using real-time data from 8+ municipal and federal sources
  • Build an interactive decision-support tool for urban planners and policymakers
  • Validate the system against real-world parking infrastructure decisions in Phoenix, AZ

💡 Key Contributions

  • Multi-Agent Pipeline — 6 specialized agents (Data Collector, Traffic Analyst, Demand Estimator, Spatial Optimizer, Policy Checker, Investment Advisor) collaborate in an 8-step pipeline
  • Real-Time Data Fusion — Integrates OSM, Census ACS, Phoenix Open Data, ADOT AADT, and Valley Metro GTFS in the browser without backend infrastructure
  • Explainable AI — Full chain-of-thought reasoning traces for every recommendation, with rejected-site rationale
  • What-If Analysis — Monte Carlo ROI simulation, sensitivity tornado charts, and before/after coverage comparison

📐 Supply Estimation Model

For each candidate site, we estimate existing parking supply within a 3.5 km radius using three complementary data layers:

  • OSM Parking Facilities — All amenity=parking features with capacity tags (when available) or estimated at 150 spaces per facility
  • Phoenix Street Meters — Official parking meter locations from the City of Phoenix ArcGIS REST API, each counted as 1 space
  • Baseline Estimate — Area-calibrated estimate using known infrastructure density (50-300 spaces per km² depending on zoning)
  • Formula: Supply = OSM_spaces + Meter_count + Baseline × area_factor

📈 Demand Estimation Model

Parking demand is modeled as a function of traffic volume, population density, commercial activity, and commute patterns:

  • Traffic Component — AADT volume × parking generation rate (5-8% of daily traffic)
  • Population Component — Resident density × vehicle ownership rate (from Census B25044)
  • Commercial Component — POI density × category-specific parking demand factors
  • Drive-Alone Rate — Census B08301 commuting data by tract
  • Formula: Demand = α·Traffic + β·Population + γ·Commercial + δ·Events

🧮 Multi-Criteria Scoring (12 Dimensions)

Each candidate site is scored across 5 weighted dimensions (user-adjustable), with 12 underlying sub-criteria:

  • Traffic Score (0-100) — AADT volume, congestion index, intersection density
  • Gap Score (0-100) — Supply-demand deficit, utilization rate, overflow frequency
  • Growth Score (0-100) — Population growth rate, new building permits, land value appreciation
  • POI Score (0-100) — Retail/dining/medical/entertainment density within 1 km
  • Transit Score (0-100) — Distance to nearest rail stop, bus route coverage, park-and-ride potential

Final score: S = w₁·Traffic + w₂·Gap + w₃·Growth + w₄·POI + w₅·Transit

📋 Policy Compliance Agent

Before final recommendation, each site passes through an automated policy check against:

  • Zoning Compliance — Verifies land use designation permits parking development (C-2, C-3, A-1/A-2 zones)
  • Environmental Review — Checks proximity to protected areas, flood zones, and heritage sites
  • ADA Accessibility — Ensures minimum accessible parking ratios per ADA standards
  • Municipal Code — Validates setback requirements, height limits, and stormwater management
